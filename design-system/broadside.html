01 — OverviewBroadside / Protest-poster
The Premise

type so large it stops being text

Display sizes at 13vw put a single word near 187px — wide enough that the eye scans letterforms before reading. One typeface (Barlow), one accent (fire orange), zero decoration. The reference is broadside printing and Wim Crouwel grids with one loud colour.

03 — TypographyBarlow + IBM Plex Mono
Lowercase Always

one face, heavy and lowercase

Barlow carries every text role from display to body — range comes from weight (400–900) and size, not face contrast. Display is never uppercase — the lowercase-shout is the system's most distinctive choice. IBM Plex Mono appears only in chrome.

05 — LayoutNegative space
Breathing Room

one massive moment, breathing

A correct slide pairs one display moment with a 1–2 line caption and nothing else. Bullet lists cap at three. Cover, chapter, statement, quote, and end slides suppress chrome entirely and leave the type alone in the field.

06 — Depth & ShapeFlat plane
No Elevation

flat, on a single plane

No drop shadows, no elevation, no rounded surfaces, no gradients. Hierarchy is built entirely from type weight, type size, and 1px hairline dividers. Border-radius is 0 everywhere except the tiny nav dots.

07 — Do & Don'tHold the fire
The Discipline

hold the fire

Do
  • Set every Barlow display moment in lowercase — the system's signature.
  • Make fire orange the only accent on dark, the whole ground on orange.
  • Use the / mono glyph in orange as the bullet; cap lists at three.
  • Apply negative tracking on display; 1px hairlines for all structure.
  • Suppress chrome on cover, chapter, statement, quote, and end.
Don't
  • Uppercase Barlow display, or pair it with a serif.
  • Introduce a second accent colour beyond fire orange.
  • Use cream headlines on orange — ink on fire is absolute.
  • Add drop shadows, rounded surfaces, or gradients.
  • Pack more than one display moment into a slide.
08 — 中文 / CJKHeavy serif declaration
Broadside, In Chinese

heavy serif, on fire

Lowercase has no CJK equivalent, so the identity shifts to 思源宋体 900 — a heavy serif at protest-poster scale reads as printed broadside more credibly than a heavy sans. The two-register colour system and fire-orange accent carry the rest.
